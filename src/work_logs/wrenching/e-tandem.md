# E-Tandem Work Log

## Project Overview
Graham had an e-bike motor laying around, and I always wanted a tandem bike, so the idea was to throw the e-bike motor onto a tandem. I bought a $200 steel frame bike incredibly clunky and old, off Facebook Marketplace. In one afternoon, Graham and I tried to mount the e-bike motor.

The stock drivetrain was the main headache: the pilot up front had a left-hand chainring that drove a left chainring on the passenger side, which then went to a traditional 3-speed on the right. The e-bike motor wants the chainline on the right, as usual. We went back and forth on design ideas and ended up putting both the front and rear chainrings on the right side, with the rear chainring carrying two chains.

## Work Log

### 07/06/2026
**Task:** First commute, chain tension issues

**Notes:**
- Instead of cutting and rewelding the tensioner, extended the length of the tensioner bolt — a little crooked, which is exactly what I was trying to avoid, but it seemed to work okay at first
- Learned that tension is huge for keeping the chain from skipping or jumping off
- Made it about two miles to work before the chain started to loosen, skip, and jump
- I properly tightened the tensioner and no issues after that!
- However the motor did want to start loosening up, it was clunking around down there
  - Graham suggested really hammering the locking ring, and installing the twop mickey mouse bolts after the locking ring which does make sense
    - There is also the option to space the drive side farther out if I wanted to, but the chain line is good now I think. main problem is the passenger pedaling at mach jesus

### 07/05/2026
**Task:** Welding BB, assembly

**Notes:**
- Bottom bracket wasn't compatible with the e-bike motor at all — had it measured, laser-cut shims made, then welded them into the bottom bracket to create a proper home for the motor
- Shims fit perfectly; simple autogenous weld
- Cable management is still horrendous. We'll need to figure that out.
